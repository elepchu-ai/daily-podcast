## [en] World news

Now the world news, starting with telecom.

The big regulatory moment for the industry lands today. The US Federal Communications Commission votes on two linked steps for satellite-to-phone service. The first is advancing an auction of twenty-five megahertz of prime mid-band spectrum. The second is asking for public comment on making another four hundred and eighty-two megahertz available for supplemental coverage from space. AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile oppose using those bands. They argue that about three hundred million people depend on that spectrum and that sharing it with satellites is not technically workable. SpaceX and Amazon are the obvious beneficiaries. For equipment makers like Ericsson and Nokia, the outcome matters because it decides how much spectrum terrestrial networks keep, and so how much new radio equipment operators will need.

Next, Qualcomm. The chipmaker reports its fiscal fourth quarter on Wednesday, the fourth of November. Its own guidance, issued in July, points to revenue between nine point seven and ten point five billion dollars. Handset revenue fell twenty percent in the last reported quarter, and analysts are watching how much the phone slowdown and Apple's shrinking share weigh against growth in cars.

Now to the economy and markets. The US Federal Reserve held interest rates yesterday at three point seven five to four percent. The vote was ten to two, and the two dissenters, Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, wanted to raise rates by a quarter point. The statement said the economy is expanding at a strong pace and that inflation remains elevated. Chair Kevin Warsh said he does not believe in forward guidance except in a crisis. Traders now see roughly an eighty-five percent chance of a rate hike on the ninth of December. The S&P five hundred still rose zero point two eight percent to seven thousand seven hundred forty-four, and the Dow added zero point one nine percent.

After the closing bell, Alphabet reported third-quarter results. Revenue rose twenty-five percent to one hundred twenty-seven point six billion dollars. Google Cloud was the star: revenue roughly doubled to thirty point seven billion dollars, operating income more than tripled to eleven point one billion, and the backlog of signed contracts reached five hundred sixty-one billion. Earnings per share came in at two dollars twelve cents, which one data provider says missed expectations, and one market report had the shares lower in extended trading.

On the war in Ukraine, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said President Zelensky is welcome to come to Moscow if he wants negotiations. Vladimir Putin also spoke by phone with the leaders of Finland and Norway about settling the conflict, and Zelensky spoke with the same two leaders separately. Russia, meanwhile, barred seven executives from British defense companies, accusing their firms of supplying Ukraine. And an analysis from the Center for European Policy Analysis says Putin has floated a three-way meeting with Xi Jinping and Donald Trump on the sidelines of the APEC summit on the eighteenth and nineteenth of November. The analysts argue the idea mostly serves Moscow.

On politics, Israel's election outcome is still uncertain a day after the vote. Exit polls showed the two blocs almost tied, so the real contest now moves to coalition talks, where a party needs sixty-one of one hundred twenty seats.

Now sport. In baseball, the Tampa Bay Rays avoided a World Series sweep, and won Game Four four to three over the Milwaukee Brewers in twelve innings. Cooper Pratt's home run put Milwaukee ahead in the top of the twelfth, but the Rays scored twice in the bottom half. Pinch hitter Ben Williamson drove in the winning run. The Brewers still lead the series three games to one, and Game Five could give them their first championship. Star Rays infielder Junior Caminero left the game with a foot injury.

In English football, Liverpool beat Chelsea on penalties at Anfield in the Carabao Cup fourth round. Chelsea's Morgan Rogers and Liverpool's Cody Gakpo each scored from the spot, and it finished one all after ninety minutes. Liverpool won the shootout six to five when Joe Gomez scored and Fofana sent his kick over the bar. Liverpool go through to the quarter-finals.

Finally, health. Reuters reports that the Ebola outbreak caused by the Bundibugyo strain in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has now passed ten thousand cases, with four thousand eight hundred eighty-seven deaths. Cases have also appeared in Kenya, Nigeria and Uganda. There is still no approved vaccine or treatment for this particular strain, which is why health agencies are treating the spread to neighboring countries so seriously.
