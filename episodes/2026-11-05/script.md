## [en] World news

Now to the world. A big night in Washington has reshaped the morning headlines, and we will start there. But first, telecom.

The biggest telecom story of the day involves a name you may not expect. Qualcomm has signed a multi-year deal to supply the radio equipment, baseband units and software for the terrestrial five G network that SpaceX plans to build for Starlink Mobile. Qualcomm called it its biggest five G deal yet. The gear is due to go up across tens of thousands of macro cell sites in the United States, starting in twenty twenty seven. Financial terms were not disclosed. The point of the network is to challenge Verizon, AT and T and T-Mobile. Analysts see an open RAN opportunity, but they still question coverage and capacity. Remember that a day earlier we heard SpaceX was weighing a purchase of Boost Mobile. This deal says the company intends to build, not only rent. It also says that the traditional radio vendors are not the only game in town.

On the Ericsson side, there is a new milestone on the road to six G. Ericsson and MediaTek say they completed the first multi-vendor pre-six G connection over the air with T-Mobile US, using a four hundred megahertz channel. That is a very wide pipe, and it shows how early six G prototypes are starting to work across different suppliers' equipment.

And in Singapore, Ericsson, Singtel and the National University Health System have expanded their partnership on connected healthcare. The work covers five G Advanced today and six G research for the future, aimed at hospitals and clinical use. A quiet story, but a real use case for the networks.

Now to the main story. Democrats swept the midterm elections. CBS projects that Democrats have secured at least fifty four Senate seats, with gains in Texas, Kansas, Ohio, Iowa, North Carolina, South Carolina and Maine. In Maine, Susan Collins lost to Troy Jackson. In Georgia, Jon Ossoff held his seat. CBS and CNBC both say Democrats also took the House. Mississippi and Alaska were still being counted. Vice President Vance said the war with Iran was the main reason Republicans lost Congress, and reports say President Trump indicated he would accept the results.

Markets loved it, or at least parts of them did. The Dow gained about fifteen hundred points, or two point nine percent, to close near fifty three thousand three hundred. That is its best day since April twenty twenty five. The S and P five hundred rose one point six percent, and the Nasdaq rose one point four percent. The real action was in oil. Traders bet that the Iran war could end, and Brent crude fell sharply, by roughly eight to nine percent, to the mid ninety dollars a barrel. Energy shares were the biggest losers. Analysts warn the move is tentative, because the diplomatic signals from Tehran remain mixed.

On Iran itself, the US struck Iran's Qeshm Island, and President Trump confirmed it on Tuesday. Even so, Qatari mediators keep passing messages. The two sides are reportedly discussing a framework: Iran would stop attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, and Washington would lift its blockade of Iranian ports. Iran says it could reopen the strait within two days if its demands are met.

On Ukraine, the Kremlin confirmed that Russia, the United States and Ukraine will hold trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on November tenth and eleventh. Envoy Steve Witkoff said the agenda covers ending the war, post-war security arrangements and de-escalation.

## [fr] L'actualité de la France

On termine avec la France, et d'abord avec le budget. Mercredi soir, l'Assemblée nationale a rejeté la partie recettes du budget 2027. Le vote a été sans appel : quatre cent cinquante-huit voix contre, aucune pour. Conséquence directe, c'est le texte initial du gouvernement qui part maintenant au Sénat, sans que les députés aient examiné la partie dépenses. Le Premier ministre Sébastien Lecornu dit toujours vouloir éviter le 49.3, mais cette option reste sur la table, avec le risque d'une motion de censure.

Aujourd'hui, c'est jour de grève et de manifestations dans toute la France. L'intersyndicale se mobilise sur les salaires et sur le budget. Selon une note du renseignement territorial datée du trois novembre, entre cinq cent mille et sept cent mille manifestants sont attendus en régions, et entre quarante mille et quatre-vingt mille à Paris. Ce sont des prévisions, pas des comptages. Près de deux cent cinquante cortèges étaient déjà recensés par la CGT. Dans l'Éducation nationale, le ministère indique que plus de dix-sept pour cent des enseignants du premier degré public ont prévu de faire grève. Les syndicats lycéens et étudiants, mobilisés depuis la mi-septembre, appellent eux aussi à défiler. Côté transports, la SNCF annonce quelques perturbations, tandis que la RATP table sur un trafic normal en Île-de-France.

Un fait divers a aussi marqué la journée de mercredi : après l'agression d'une directrice de maternelle à Nice, franceinfo rapporte que la suspecte faisait l'objet d'une obligation de quitter le territoire français. Une enquête pour tentative de meurtre est ouverte. Sur les réseaux sociaux, des appels au meurtre visant des livreurs ont été constatés, ce qui a provoqué de vives réactions.

Enfin, une information côté culture : l'émission Groland s'arrêtera le douze décembre, après trente-quatre ans d'antenne.
