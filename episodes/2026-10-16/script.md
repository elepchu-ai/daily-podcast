## [vi] Tin tức trong nước

Xin chào Elep, chào mừng bạn đến với bản tin buổi sáng thứ Sáu, ngày mười sáu tháng Mười năm hai nghìn không trăm hai mươi sáu. Chúc bạn một chuyến đi làm thật thuận lợi. Chúng ta bắt đầu với tin trong nước.

Trước hết là chính trị. Hội nghị lần thứ tư Ban Chấp hành Trung ương Đảng khóa mười bốn đã bế mạc chiều tám tháng Mười tại Hà Nội, sau bốn ngày làm việc. Hội nghị thông qua ba nghị quyết, về tăng cường xây dựng, chỉnh đốn Đảng và hệ thống chính trị, về đổi mới phương thức lãnh đạo, cầm quyền của Đảng, và về xây dựng đội ngũ cán bộ các cấp. Hội nghị cũng bầu bổ sung đồng chí Trần Quang Bảo vào Ủy ban Kiểm tra Trung ương, và đồng ý đổi tên Đảng ủy Mặt trận Tổ quốc, các đoàn thể Trung ương thành Đảng ủy Dân vận, Mặt trận và các đoàn thể Trung ương. Ngày mai, mười bảy tháng Mười, Quốc hội sẽ khai mạc kỳ họp cuối năm.

Sang chính sách y tế. Bộ Y tế đang lấy ý kiến về dự thảo nghị định tiến tới cấm hút thuốc hoàn toàn trong nhà tại khách sạn, quán bar và karaoke. Hiện nay các nơi này vẫn được bố trí khu vực dành riêng cho người hút thuốc. Dự thảo cũng đề xuất cấm tuyệt đối thuốc lá điện tử và nâng diện tích cảnh báo sức khỏe trên bao bì lên ít nhất tám mươi lăm phần trăm. Nội dung này nằm trong hồ sơ sửa đổi luật phòng, chống tác hại của thuốc lá, dự kiến trình Quốc hội tại kỳ họp cuối năm, và luật sửa đổi có thể có hiệu lực từ ngày một tháng Bảy năm hai nghìn không trăm hai mươi bảy.

Về kinh tế và du lịch. Chín tháng đầu năm, Việt Nam đón hơn mười bảy phẩy bảy triệu lượt khách quốc tế, tăng mười bốn phẩy năm phần trăm so với cùng kỳ năm ngoái. Riêng tháng Chín có khoảng một phẩy bảy bảy triệu lượt, tăng mười sáu phần trăm, là mức cao nhất trong năm năm cho tháng Chín. Khách đến bằng đường hàng không chiếm hơn tám mươi ba phần trăm, và thị trường châu Âu tăng mạnh nhất, khoảng năm mươi lăm phần trăm. Con số này đã đạt gần bảy mươi mốt phần trăm mục tiêu hai mươi lăm triệu lượt khách của cả năm.

Một tin vui cho người lao động. Chính phủ đã đồng ý phương án nghỉ Tết Nguyên đán năm hai nghìn không trăm hai mươi bảy kéo dài bảy ngày liên tục, từ ngày bốn đến ngày mười tháng Hai, tức từ hai mươi tám tháng Chạp đến hết mùng năm tháng Giêng. Kỳ nghỉ Quốc khánh năm sau cũng kéo dài bốn ngày. Nhiều gia đình có thể bắt đầu lên kế hoạch về quê và đi chơi sớm từ bây giờ.

Về thiên tai. Thủy điện Trị An, hồ thủy điện lớn nhất miền Nam, tiếp tục tăng xả lũ qua đập tràn trong ngày mười lăm tháng Mười. Người dân ven sông Đồng Nai và khu vực hạ du nên theo dõi thông báo của đơn vị vận hành và cơ quan khí tượng thủy văn.

Cuối cùng là thị trường. Chốt phiên mười lăm tháng Mười, VN-Index giảm sáu phẩy năm hai điểm, tương đương không phẩy ba tám phần trăm, xuống còn một nghìn sáu trăm chín mươi tám phẩy ba lăm điểm, sau bảy phiên giảm liên tiếp. ## [en] World news

Now to the world news. We start with technology and artificial intelligence, where the big story is debt.

Shares of CoreWeave, the AI cloud company, were hit hard this week as investors worried about how the AI build-out is being paid for. The company carries around fifty billion dollars in debt. The cost of insuring that debt rose to roughly one thousand basis points, which implies close to a sixty percent chance of default within five years, up from about thirty percent a year ago. Bonds it issued in April, paying nine point seven five percent, traded as low as eighty-one cents on the dollar. Reports differed on the size of the stock's drop, between fifteen and seventeen percent on Wednesday, and neocloud peer Nebius fell about eleven percent. The chief executive called the selloff irrational and pointed to the company's backlog of orders. Why it matters: analysts say this is less about weak demand and more about funding, and it helped trigger Thursday's rout in Asian tech shares.

Second, a different kind of AI news. Nvidia announced a five-year compute donation worth two hundred fifty million dollars to strengthen the United Kingdom's research on the safety and security of AI agents. Separately, the American Federal Trade Commission has sent OpenAI and Anthropic detailed demands, about fifty pages each, for records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy. Anthropic also opened an office in Singapore and signed a memorandum with the Singapore government. Taken together, these stories show two trends at once. Governments are paying for AI safety work, while regulators want to see what the leading labs know about problems with their own systems. Expect more scrutiny, not less, as agents gain access to real company data. For listeners following the industry, the practical point is that safety funding and regulatory pressure are now arriving in the same week, and the labs will have to answer to both. The compute donation will be spread over five years, so its effect will be gradual rather than immediate.

Turning to geopolitics. Russia launched what officials called a massive attack on Ukraine on Wednesday, killing at least nine people and damaging power sites, including a heating and power plant in Kharkiv. Earlier in the week a strike on an apartment block in Kharkiv killed four people. President Zelensky accused Moscow of violating the so-called energy ceasefire that President Trump announced on Sunday, an arrangement neither government has formally confirmed. Ukraine, for its part, hit the Ryazan oil refinery in Russia on Thursday. Why it matters: the energy truce was meant to protect civilians ahead of winter, and its fragility shows how far apart the two sides remain. Washington is also reportedly pressing European states on frozen Russian assets as leverage in peace talks. Kyiv's mayor said further attacks hit energy sites, and parts of the capital lost power, which means families face the first cold nights with unreliable heating. Casualty figures from the latest strike are early and may still change.

Second geopolitics story, from the Black Sea. Bulgaria summoned the Russian ambassador on Tuesday after drone strikes hit two cargo ships off its coast. One ship sank and eleven sailors are missing. It was the second deadly attack in about a week, after an earlier strike killed ten crew members, mostly Syrians. On Thursday, two tankers also caught fire off Romania's coast, with seven people missing. The Bulgarian prime minister said there was no clear proof of who was responsible, though he argued the logic points to Russia, which has denied involvement. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte met Bulgarian and Romanian leaders on Thursday about the rising number of attacks. Why it matters: commercial shipping near two NATO members is now under repeated fire.

On the economy, inflation in the United States came in hotter than expected. Consumer prices rose three point seven percent in September from a year earlier, against forecasts of three point six. Core inflation, which excludes food and energy, was two point six percent. Producer prices jumped one point three percent in the month, far above the point three percent expected. Traders now see a higher chance that the Federal Reserve raises rates rather than holds at its October twenty-eighth meeting, though most still expect no change. The reaction was fastest in Seoul. South Korea's Kospi index fell about seven and a half percent on Thursday, closing near five thousand five hundred fifty points, with Samsung Electronics down more than eight percent and SK Hynix down nearly eleven percent. Foreign investors have sold more than ten billion dollars of Korean shares this month, and financial authorities in Seoul held an emergency meeting. Why it matters: when inflation stays high, central banks cannot cut rates, and expensive borrowing is exactly what heavily indebted AI companies fear most.

On society, a tragedy in Pakistan. The roof of a private school in Peshawar collapsed on Thursday, killing at least thirty-five people, most of them children aged six to twelve, and injuring more than fifty. Rescuers, almost one hundred of them, have pulled everyone from the rubble. Police arrested the school's owner and say the building was structurally weak. The owner blames recent rains. A committee will investigate. It is the second deadly building failure involving children in Pakistan this year, after a collapse at a tuition centre in Lahore in June killed fourteen. Why it matters: it renews pressure on authorities to enforce building safety rules for schools, which are often privately run and lightly inspected.

Now sport. In the Champions League on Wednesday night, the holders Paris Saint-Germain beat Manchester City four goals to two at the Etihad. Doué, Dembélé and Vitinha put PSG three up before halftime, and Akliouche restored the lead after Erling Haaland scored twice, becoming City's all-time top scorer in the competition. Real Madrid came back from three goals to one down in Rome to win four to three at Roma, with three goals in the last eight minutes. Liverpool won six to three at LASK, with a hat-trick from Bradley Barcola, and Aston Villa beat Fenerbahçe six nil, their biggest European win. In all, forty-six goals were scored across nine matches, a league-phase record.

In tennis, the Shanghai Masters has reached the quarterfinals. Daniil Medvedev beat Stefanos Tsitsipas six four, seven five without facing a break point. Alex de Minaur beat Karen Khachanov six three, six four. The last-eight lineup lists Medvedev against Tommy Paul, Zverev against de Minaur, and the young Spaniard Rafael Jódar against Casper Ruud. The top two names are missing: Jannik Sinner is still out with a knee injury, and Carlos Alcaraz went out earlier. The final is on Sunday, so the title looks wide open. Shanghai is also a test of stamina, since the tournament runs for two weeks and several players have already battled through long three-set matches. Zverev, for example, needed two tiebreaks to get past Alexander Bublik in the fourth round. Keep an eye on Jódar, who is making a run at one of the biggest events of the season.

Now health. Pope Leo the Fourteenth had surgery in Rome on Tuesday and the Vatican says he has early-stage lung cancer. Surgeons removed the lower lobe of his right lung in a camera-guided operation lasting about ninety minutes, with no complications. The diagnosis is adenocarcinoma, and oncologists describe the outlook as excellent because it was caught early. He is walking and doing breathing therapy, and audiences are cancelled until at least October twentieth. On the research side, Nature Medicine published a phase one trial this week of personalized vaccines, built from each patient's own tumor mutations, combined with an immunotherapy drug in ovarian cancer. It was safe and feasible, and produced tumor-specific immune responses in many patients. It is early, small and not yet proof of survival benefit, but it points toward tailored treatment.

Finally, science. NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope was named one of Time magazine's best inventions of the year this week. Earlier this month, NASA reported that Roman had shown remarkable pointing stability and that its coronagraph, an instrument that blocks starlight to image faint planets, had taken its first look at the cosmos. Why it matters: the coronagraph is a technology demonstration for finding and imaging planets around other stars, a step toward the future search for Earth-like worlds. Roman also carries a wide-field camera built to study dark energy and map huge areas of sky. The telescope launched recently, and these first results suggest that its instruments are performing well. Scientists expect years of data that will help explain why the expansion of the universe is speeding up, one of the biggest open questions in physics.

## [fr] L'actualité de la France

Passons maintenant à l'actualité de la France, ce vendredi seize octobre.

D'abord la politique. Le budget pour deux mille vingt-sept est examiné depuis mardi à l'Assemblée nationale. Les socialistes avaient déposé une motion de rejet, qui n'a pas été adoptée, et l'examen de la première partie, consacrée aux recettes, se poursuit. En commission, le vendredi neuf octobre, les députés avaient rejeté cette partie par trente et une voix contre trois. Selon le rapporteur général, les mesures adoptées auraient creusé le déficit d'environ dix milliards d'euros. Le gouvernement vise un déficit de cinq pour cent du produit intérieur brut en deux mille vingt-sept, alors que le texte issu de la commission le porterait autour de cinq virgule trois cinq pour cent. Le parti Horizons propose de son côté un budget alternatif avec dix milliards d'économies supplémentaires. La navette parlementaire sera longue et incertaine, et le gouvernement devra sans doute chercher des majorités texte par texte.

Côté économie, les chiffres restent moroses. Selon les dernières prévisions de l'Insee et de la Banque de France, la croissance française ne dépasserait pas zéro virgule quatre pour cent cette année. Au deuxième trimestre, le produit intérieur brut a stagné, après un recul au premier. Le pouvoir d'achat des ménages a baissé de zéro virgule six pour cent, et le taux de chômage est monté à huit virgule trois pour cent. Les prévisionnistes évoquent un ralentissement maîtrisé plutôt qu'une récession, mais ils attendent un chômage encore en hausse d'ici la fin de l'année. C'est dans ce contexte que s'inscrit la bataille budgétaire.

Sur le plan de la société, l'Assemblée nationale a adopté mardi, par cinq cent trois voix pour, aucune contre et soixante-sept abstentions, la proposition de loi intégrale contre les violences sexistes et sexuelles. Le texte, présenté il y a un an avec soixante-dix-huit mesures, en compte désormais cent trente-trois. Il prévoit notamment une police spécialisée et des enquêtes accélérées. La France insoumise s'est abstenue, et le Rassemblement national menace de saisir le Conseil constitutionnel si certaines dispositions, comme un congé pour les victimes, sont maintenues. Les sénateurs examineront le texte à partir de janvier. Autre fait du jour, un minibus transportant des chauffeurs routiers a percuté une navette ferroviaire au terminal Eurotunnel de Coquelles, près de Calais, jeudi en début de soirée : dix-neuf blessés, sans pronostic vital engagé selon les autorités, et une enquête judiciaire est ouverte.

Place à la culture. La Cinémathèque française consacre depuis le sept octobre une grande exposition et une rétrospective de trente-cinq films à Jean-Paul Belmondo, visibles jusqu'au trente et un janvier. Samedi, le Faust de Murnau revient sur grand écran avec un accompagnement en direct aux ondes Martenot. Et côté littérature, l'Académie Goncourt a annoncé le six octobre sa deuxième sélection de huit romans. Les finalistes seront connus le vingt-sept octobre, et le lauréat sera proclamé le trois novembre chez Drouant. Sur le cinéma, le nouveau film de Fred Cavayé, adaptation des Misérables, est sorti en salles mercredi.

Passons au sport, avec deux affiches européennes. Mercredi soir, le Paris Saint-Germain, tenant du titre, s'est imposé quatre buts à deux à Manchester City en Ligue des champions. Doué a ouvert le score dès la treizième minute, Dembélé et Vitinha ont porté le score à trois à zéro avant la pause, puis Akliouche a redonné trois buts d'avance en seconde période, malgré un doublé d'Erling Haaland. C'est la première défaite de City depuis le Community Shield. Le PSG, qui se déplace samedi à Strasbourg en championnat, arrive en pleine confiance, et tout le vestiaire parisien savoure cette victoire de prestige en Angleterre.

Enfin, jeudi soir en Ligue Europa, l'Olympique lyonnais a battu Crystal Palace trois à deux au Groupama Stadium. Tolisso a ouvert le score, Nuamah a doublé la mise avant la mi-temps, et Šulc a redonné deux buts d'avance en fin de match, après le retour des Anglais grâce à Pino et Kamada. Lyon grimpe à six points, et retrouve contre son ancien entraîneur Pierre Sage une victoire précieuse, tandis que Palace reste à trois points.
