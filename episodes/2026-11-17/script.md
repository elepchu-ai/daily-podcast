## [en] World news

Good morning. Let's start with telecom, then move to markets and the wider world.

Ericsson first. The company reported its weekly share buyback on Monday. Between the ninth and the thirteenth of November, it bought one hundred thousand shares every trading day on Nasdaq Stockholm, at prices between about one hundred and three and one hundred and seven Swedish kronor. That is part of a buyback program of up to fifteen billion kronor announced in April. It is a routine filing, and a clue to how the company is using its cash.

Now to Nokia, which had a busy Monday. One New Zealand selected Nokia's autonomous network software, aiming to move toward AI-native operations while keeping its data on its own premises. Nokia and the Nordic operator Telia are also working on a consortium called AI Gigafactory Northern, which would spread artificial intelligence computing across several sites, including existing telecom locations. The common thread is that operators and vendors are pitching networks as places to run AI, not only to carry traffic.

On the competitive picture, analysts at Dell'Oro estimate that Huawei, Ericsson, Nokia, ZTE and Samsung together held about ninety-six percent of the global radio network market in the first half of this year, with Huawei in first place.

Now to markets. Wall Street had a rough Monday. The Nasdaq fell two point five percent, to twenty-four thousand five hundred and ten. The S and P five hundred lost one point nine percent, closing at seven thousand four hundred and seventeen. It was the third losing session in a row, and the Nasdaq is now more than ten percent below its record close from October, which is the usual definition of a correction.

Chip stocks led the slide. Nvidia closed at about one hundred and fifty-six dollars, down more than four percent, ahead of its quarterly results due on Tuesday. The selling was made worse by new interim rules from Washington that tighten China's access to advanced artificial intelligence chips. The timing matters, because President Donald Trump travels to China this week for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, on the eighteenth and nineteenth of November. The Kremlin has said Vladimir Putin will attend, and a three-way meeting with Xi Jinping has been discussed, though Beijing has not confirmed one.

In Iran, the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz continues. Tehran says the strait stays closed until Washington meets all of its conditions, and an Iranian official says there are seven of them. Shipping incidents are still being reported, and there is no deal yet.

Now sport. In the National Football League, the Detroit Lions beat the New England Patriots thirty-five to ten in Munich on Sunday, in front of a record Munich crowd of more than seventy thousand people. New England led three to nothing, then Detroit scored twenty-eight unanswered points. Running back Jahmyr Gibbs had two hundred and fifty-two yards from scrimmage and two touchdowns, and Amon-Ra St. Brown, who has German roots, also starred. It was the fifth straight win for the Lions, who go to seven wins and two losses and lead the NFC North. Two injuries clouded the night: Patriots quarterback Drake Maye hurt his ankle, and Lions pass rusher Aidan Hutchinson hurt his knee.

On the science and health side, a study published this week in The Lancet Digital Health, from Mount Sinai, found that commercial drug advertising can shape the treatment suggestions that artificial intelligence tools give to users. As more people ask chatbots about their health, the authors say that safeguards against this kind of influence matter. Separately, an update published in the Journal of the American Medical Association shows five-year survival for children with cancer rising from about six in ten in nineteen seventy-five to nearly nine in ten by twenty seventeen.

That is the world for today. Now, to France.

## [fr] L'actualité de la France

Bonjour. En France, ce mardi est une journée de mobilisation dans la fonction publique. Une intersyndicale appelle à la grève et à des manifestations pour réclamer des hausses de salaires et plus de moyens pour les services publics. La CGT parle d'une nouvelle grande journée et juge que le gouvernement n'a annoncé que des miettes.

Le point de crispation, c'est le budget deux mille vingt-sept, qui prévoit de geler le point d'indice des fonctionnaires. Le SNES-FSU critique aussi une proposition de la Cour des comptes d'une année blanche, c'est-à-dire un gel des avancements et des promotions. Les syndicats de la fonction publique dénoncent en outre leur mise à l'écart de la conférence sociale organisée à Matignon. Pour mesurer l'ampleur du mouvement, il faudra attendre les chiffres du ministère, donnés en milieu de journée. Lors de la grève du cinq novembre, ils avaient fait état de près de dix pour cent de grévistes dans la fonction publique d'État, et de plus de douze pour cent dans l'Éducation nationale.

Côté gouvernement, Sébastien Lecornu a annoncé une hausse de un virgule six pour cent du Smic, qui passerait à mille cinq cent deux euros brut par mois dès décembre. Il la présente comme la deuxième hausse la plus importante en vingt ans. Les syndicats jugent la réponse insuffisante.

Au Parlement, le projet de budget de la Sécurité sociale est désormais entre les mains du Sénat, après son rejet par l'Assemblée nationale. Les sénateurs en débattent dans les prochains jours.
